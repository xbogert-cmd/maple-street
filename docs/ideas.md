## 2. New ideas to choose from

### My top six

If I had to pick, these give the most game for the least work, in this order.

| # | Idea | Why | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cut the telephone line** | Snip a house's line at the outside box before a job. Nobody inside can phone the police; they have to run to a neighbour, which buys you time. The cut is found next day | S |
| 2 | **Stakeout and an annotated street map** | Sit at your window or in your car with binoculars and let time run. Everything you see (who left when, which lights, which dog) is drawn onto a hand-sketched map of the street in your notebook | M |
| 3 | **Fixed weekly habits** | Church on Sunday morning empties half the street for two hours. Thursday is bowling night. Friday is poker at one house. Routines you can learn and plan a week around | S |
| 4 | **Away on holiday, and house-sitting** | A family leaves for a week. Papers pile up on the step. If they like you they ask you to water the plants and hand you a key, which is legitimate access to a target's house | M |
| 5 | **Frame a neighbour** | Plant contraband in someone else's house and tip off the Bureau. The investigator goes after them and heat on you drops. It can go wrong if you are seen, and it is a nasty thing to do to someone you chatted with | M |
| 6 | **Notes from the last agent** | When an agent is caught, the next one finds a few of their notes hidden in the house: a neighbour's secret, a warning about a dog. Permadeath stays, but each run leaves something behind | S |

### Tradecraft

| Idea | What it adds | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Signal marks | A chalk mark on a lamp post tells the Centre a drop is loaded or that you are in trouble. Forget it and the drop sits uncollected | S |
| Losing a tail | When the investigator follows you, a drop made in his sight is compromised. Shake him first: through the market, round the park | M |
| Safe combinations | Safes open with a number found elsewhere in the house (a birthday on a card, a note in a wallet), as the quiet alternative to cracking | S |
| More tools | Glass cutter (quiet, leaves a hole), crowbar (fast, loud), sleeping pills for a dog's dinner, chloroform for a silent takedown from the front | M |
| Forged pass | Make a plant pass at your bench to get into the restricted wing during a shift | M |
| Alibis | Being seen somewhere public at the time of an incident takes suspicion off you afterwards | S |
| Photographing people | Use the camera on the street: who visits whom, the affair in the car. Photos make leaning on someone far more likely to work | M |

### The street

| Idea | What it adds | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Regular callers | A mailman, a paperboy at dawn, a milkman. Moving witnesses on fixed rounds, and a mail delivery you can intercept | M |
| Friendships | Neighbours you chat with often become friends: they vouch for you to the investigator, and argue with neighbours who suspect you | M |
| Your own upkeep | Mow the lawn, put the bins out on Tuesday, pay bills. A neglected house draws eyes. Keeps daytime busy | S |
| Seasons and holidays | Halloween (a mask is a free disguise for one night), Fourth of July fireworks (noise cover), Christmas lights, snow | L |
| Power cut | A storm takes the street's lights out for an evening. Total darkness, everyone home, candles in windows | S |
| A second sleeper | One neighbour also works for the Centre, or for a rival service. You find each other by signals, and may be ordered to help or remove them | L |
| Cats and friendly dogs | A dog you feed every day stops barking at you for good. A cat that follows you into a house you are robbing | S |

### The plant

| Idea | What it adds | Size |
|---|---|---|
| A bigger plant | A restricted wing, guard routes, a vault. Jobs that take place at work, where everyone knows your face | L |
| Night shift | A promotion (or a favour) moves you to nights: fewer people, more access, but your nights on the street are gone | M |
| Security review | When heat is high the plant interviews staff. Your answers have to match what neighbours have said about you | M |

### Pressure and story

| Idea | What it adds | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Hard orders | The Centre tells you to burn an asset, or to deal with a neighbour who knows too much. Refusing costs their trust | M |
| Defecting | Walk up to the dark sedan and offer a deal. A different ending, and a different game if it carries on as a double agent | L |
| Your own tells | The counter-agent learns habits. The same route to the park every night makes him sure faster, so you have to vary it | S |
| Ranked endings | Leaving town is scored: a medal and a flat in Moscow, a quiet pension, or abandoned at the border | S |
| Difficulty settings | Sharper or duller neighbours, longer or shorter days, permadeath off for a first run | S |

### Home and comfort

| Idea | What it adds | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Basement and false wall | A real hidden room for the radio, the bench and a corkboard of the street with photos and string | M |
| Police scanner | Hear when a call goes out and where the patrol car is heading | S |
| Driving | Take the car to town: a bar, the bowling alley, the motel. Tail a neighbour to see where they go on Thursday afternoons | L |
| A second person in the house | Already in the design as "later": a partner agent as lookout, or a family who must never know | L |
